## The three fee numbers people quote

OKX's global regular tier charges 0.08% maker and 0.10% taker on spot. Binance's standard global spot rate is 0.10% on both sides. Pay eligible spot fees with BNB and Binance cuts that by 25%, to 0.075%. Those are the three figures behind most of the okx vs binance fee debate.

Perpetuals start in the same place. The common regular tier for USDⓈ-margined contracts is 0.02% maker and 0.05% taker on both exchanges. Binance takes another 10% off when the fee is paid in BNB, giving 0.018% and 0.045%.

What the headline numbers hide is that OKX runs separate schedules per entity. The 0.08% is the global one, and it is not what a US or EEA account sees.

## What the maker discount is worth in dollars

A $1,000 limit order that rests on the book costs $0.80 on OKX at the regular global tier and $1.00 on Binance. Route the Binance fee through BNB and it becomes $0.75. The saving over OKX works out to $0.05.

To bank $10 of that saving you need $200,000 of maker volume. A 2% move against a $500 BNB balance is also $10, and it can happen in an afternoon. The discount only pays for people who already hold BNB for other reasons or trade volume large enough that five basis points compounds: at $5 million a month in maker fills, $0.05 per $1,000 becomes $250.

On futures the arithmetic is easier to see. Open and close a $10,000 position at market and the taker fee is $5 a side, $10 round trip on either exchange at 0.05%. With BNB it is $9 on Binance. Funding payments and slippage on a position that size will usually exceed the $1 difference.

## Liquidity: one number that gets misread, and two third-party tests

A widely repeated figure comes from a Binance sample taken on 29 August 2026, which measured about $5.78 million of BTC/USDT bids and $6.50 million of asks within 10 basis points of the midpoint. There is no same-time OKX measurement behind it. It is one side of a comparison, not a verdict on execution quality, and treating it as proof that Binance fills better would be reading more into the data than it holds.

Coin Bureau's 1 August 2026 review did test both platforms in the same markets and found Binance deeper in BTC, ETH, SOL and ONDO, with OKX competitive on majors and thinner in those specific books. Token Metrics, writing on 8 July 2026, put Binance's order books at the top of the industry for standard futures and spot, and named OKX's options market as the place where OKX wins, with depth that rivals Deribit and exceeds what Binance offers.

If you trade options, that last point carries more weight than the spot fee gap. If you trade size in BTC perpetuals, it doesn't.
